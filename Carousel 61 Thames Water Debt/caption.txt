📌 Thames Water. £18bn in debt. Half the country's water going through its pipes.

📚 Thames Water borrowed to fund infrastructure, paid dividends to shareholders while the pipes aged, and now cannot service the debt without either government support or a price rise customers cannot afford.

📈 GCSE lens (2.1 business finance): excessive debt raises interest payments and reduces the cash available for operations. A-Level lens (2.3.4 financial ratios / 3.6 external influences): gearing above 50% signals high financial risk. Regulators set the price cap, so the business cannot simply raise revenue.

⚠️ Regulated utilities cannot raise prices freely. That is the constraint that makes Thames Water a special case. Most businesses with high debt can cut costs or raise prices. Thames Water needed approval for both.

💾 Debt is useful until it isn't. The evaluation is always: at what level does the benefit of leverage become the risk of insolvency?
