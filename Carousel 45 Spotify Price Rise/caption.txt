📌 Spotify raised its prices again. Subscribers stayed. Revenue went up.

📚 That is price inelastic demand. When price rises and revenue rises with it, the percentage fall in quantity was smaller than the percentage rise in price. Spotify's switching costs are high: playlists, algorithms, podcasts. The alternative is rebuilding all of that somewhere else.

📈 GCSE lens (1.4.3 price): businesses with strong brand loyalty can raise prices without losing many customers. A-Level lens (1.2.4 PED): inelastic demand gives pricing power. The question is how far you can push it before substitutes start to look worth the switch.

⚠️ Price inelasticity is not permanent. If Apple Music or YouTube Premium closes the gap on features, the switching cost falls and demand becomes more elastic.

💾 Spotify is a textbook PED example. The extract numbers will tell you which direction demand went. Use those.
