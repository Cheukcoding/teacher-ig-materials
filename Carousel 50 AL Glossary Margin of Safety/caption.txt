📌 Margin of safety. Easy to calculate. Harder to use correctly in an evaluate question.

📚 Definition: the difference between actual or forecast output and the break-even output. Margin of safety = current output - break-even output. It shows how far sales can fall before the business starts making a loss.

📈 A large margin of safety means the business has room to absorb a drop in demand. A small margin means even a small fall in sales pushes the business into a loss.

⚠️ The exam trap: students calculate the margin of safety correctly and then never use it. The number needs to be in a chain: "with a margin of safety of only 200 units, a 5% fall in demand would push the business into a loss, therefore..."

💾 Margin of safety earns marks when it sits inside a risk assessment, not just as a standalone calculation.
