📌 Cocoa hit record prices. Chocolate manufacturers passed some of it on, absorbed the rest, and quietly shrank the bar.

📚 Shrinkflation: the same price, a smaller product. The manufacturer keeps revenue stable while reducing costs per unit. Most consumers do not notice immediately, and the price point stays familiar.

📈 GCSE lens (1.5.4 inflation and 2.2.3 pricing): cost-push inflation forces businesses to choose between raising prices, reducing quality or accepting lower margins. A-Level lens (1.3.3 pricing): shrinkflation maintains the psychological price point while compressing costs.

⚠️ Shrinkflation is a short-run strategy. Once consumers notice, brand trust takes a hit. The long-run evaluation is whether that trust damage costs more than a transparent price rise would have.

💾 Input cost rises are a Section B extract every year. The question is always: who absorbs it, and how?
