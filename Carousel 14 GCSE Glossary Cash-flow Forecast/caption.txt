📌 Cash-flow forecast. One of the most misread terms on the GCSE paper.

📚 Definition: a prediction of the cash coming in and going out of a business over a future period, usually month by month. It shows whether the business will have enough cash at any point, not whether it will make a profit.

📈 Profit and cash are not the same thing. A business can be profitable and run out of cash, if customers pay late or stock is bought in bulk. The forecast shows timing, not just totals.

⚠️ Common exam trap: a student writes about profit when the question asks about cash flow. The examiner marks what the question asks, not what the student knows.

💾 Net cash flow = cash in - cash out. Opening balance + net cash flow = closing balance. The closing balance is what the bank cares about.
