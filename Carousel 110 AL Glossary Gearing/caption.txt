📌 Gearing. The ratio that tells you how much of a business's funding comes from borrowing versus shareholders.

📚 Formula: (non-current liabilities / total capital employed) x 100. High gearing (above 50%): most funding from debt. Low gearing: mostly shareholder equity. A highly geared business pays more in interest and is more vulnerable when revenue falls.

📈 In evaluation: high gearing is a problem when interest rates rise or when revenue falls. A business with high gearing and low revenue growth is trapped: cannot invest because cash goes to debt repayment. A business with high gearing but strong, predictable revenue (a utility) manages the risk better.

⚠️ Gearing is not bad on its own. Debt is cheaper than equity finance if the business can service it. The evaluation is always: can this business manage this level of debt given its revenue stability?
