📌 The government stepped in to save British Steel. Public ownership, emergency legislation, thousands of jobs.

📚 British Steel's Scunthorpe blast furnaces were days from being shut permanently by their Chinese owner. Once a blast furnace is shut cold, restarting it costs hundreds of millions. The government chose to intervene rather than lose the capacity.

📈 GCSE lens (1.5.4 government influence): governments intervene when a business failure would cause public costs too large to ignore, including unemployment and strategic supply loss. A-Level lens (3.6 political and legal): state aid rules, foreign ownership regulation and industrial strategy are all external constraints on what businesses can do.

⚠️ Public ownership solves the immediate closure risk but not the underlying economics. Steel produced in the UK still costs more than imported steel. The business model problem remains.

💾 Government intervention in business is a built-in evaluation point: short-run solution, long-run uncertainty.
